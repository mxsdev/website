Terrence Tao recently coined this new era as a Copernican revolution in intelligence. Apparently, intelligence was not as distinctively human or supreme as we once thought. How astonished I was to hear this! I had read the same thing in Nietzsche, writing over a century ago:

> _In some remote corner of the universe, poured out and glittering in countless solar systems, there was once a star on which clever beasts invented knowing. That was the most arrogant and mendacious minute of 'world history'_

Nietzsche understood that we are far, far more than _intellects_ — and in so many of our activities, intellect plays a surprisingly small part. A striking proposition: so we humans are _displaced_ — what if now as _less_ impoverished?

Software engineering is now an arbitrage — it is almost certain that most senior "humans in the loop" are embarrassingly less valuable than they are being paid. It is the worst time possible to optimize for compensation and titles, because we have no clue what they will be in the next few years. If our engineers want a new _task_, it should be this — to use this ephemeral time to find a new voice compatible with this new future.

What such new voices are available? The pro-AI voices are quite right that the anti-AI crowd is largely living in fear and burying their heads in the sand; but perhaps it _takes one to know one_. Is impetuously driving to rewrite everything in uncertain and untested technology any less driven by…_fear_? And fear of the same thing! What is certain: grand outcomes will precipitate no less from grand _courage_ in this new era than from the old one; and grand courage has no less its characteristic symptoms of _calm and steadiness_, a scarce commodity today.

At the heart equally of AI doomerism and fanaticism is a profound misunderstanding: "AI" is not a product, in itself! Treating "AI" as a product involves foreclosing any possible advantage a company could have _even in principle_; it is letting the instrument master _us_, lest we take the effort to instrument _it_ — and perhaps this instrumentation requires a hitherto undervalued kind of effort for our engineers: _artistry and craft_.

Could there be style in _types and contracts_? This one will produce ingenious _invariants_ that solve these problems of security and determinism, as poets affect solemnity through the austerity of their prose. An artist of elegant constraints!

Could there be style in the _orchestration of agents_? It is the style of a great rhetorician, that can mediate and break up any argument, without either side feeling as though they _lost_. Our new politicians, our new _engineers of deliberation_.

Could there be style in _representing our physical world_? They say art imitates nature after all! And is one's _purpose_ not inseparable from one's _style_? "Context preparation is critical" — is this not an engineer's way of saying: it is beneficial to have more styles than realism! Our new stylists, our _modeling engineers_.

Could there be style in _distillation of business knowledge_? "Code is dead" misunderstands that it is the _web of human concerns the code represents_, and not the text on the screen, from which value is born. But this is a web! And a web is an _organic_ mess — and need I remind you that not even our greatest robots can _unwind knots_? I welcome our new knitters, our new embroiderers…

Oh, look around! Are there not so many new portraits ready to be fashioned? But so few _painters_. Perhaps the best portrait I can provide, is that of the engineer that _paints their own portrait_ — with enough of these, we may one day look forward to a new beautiful _landscape_. And you're afraid of this?!
