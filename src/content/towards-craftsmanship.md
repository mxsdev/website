Terrence Tao recently coined this new era as a Copernican revolution in intelligence. Apparently, intelligence was not as distinctively human or supreme as we once thought. How astonished I was to hear this! I had read the same thing in Nietzsche, writing over a century ago:

> *In some remote corner of the universe, poured out and glittering in countless solar systems, there was once a star on which clever beasts invented knowing. That was the most arrogant and mendacious minute of 'world history,'*

Nietzsche understood that we are far, far more than *intellects* — and in so many of our activities, intellect plays a surprisingly small part. A striking proposition: so we humans are *displaced* — what if now as *less* impoverished?

Software engineering is now an arbitrage — it is almost certain that most senior "humans in the loop" are embarrassingly less valuable than they are being paid. It is the worst time possible to optimize for compensation and titles, because we have no clue what they will be in the next few years. If our engineers want a new *task*, it should be this — to use this ephemeral time to find a new voice compatible with this new future.

What such new voices are available? The pro-AI voices are quite right that the anti-AI crowd is largely living in fear and burying their heads in the sand; but perhaps it *takes one to know one*. Is impetuously driving to rewrite everything in uncertain and untested technology any less driven by…*fear*? And fear of the same thing! What is certain: grand outcomes will precipitate no less from grand *courage* in this new era than from the old one; and grand courage has no less its characteristic symptoms of *calm and steadiness*, a scarce commodity today.

At the heart equally of AI doomerism and fanaticism is a profound misunderstanding: "AI" is not a product, in itself! Treating "AI" as a product involves foreclosing any possible advantage a company could have *even in principle*; it is letting the instrument master *us*, lest we take the effort to instrument *it* — and perhaps this instrumentation requires a hitherto undervalued kind of effort for our engineers: *artistry and craft*.

Could there be style in *types and contracts*? This one will produce ingenious *invariants* that solve these problems of security and determinism, as poets affect solemnity through the austerity of their prose. An artist of elegant constraints!

Could there be style in the *orchestration of agents*? It is the style of a great rhetorician, that can mediate and break up any argument, without either side feeling as though they *lost*. Our new politicians, our new *engineers of deliberation*.

Could there be style in *representing our physical world*? They say art imitates nature after all! And is one's *purpose* not inseparable from one's *style*? "Context preparation is critical" — is this not an engineer's way of saying: it is beneficial to have more styles than realism! Our new stylists, our *modeling engineers*.

Could there be style in *distillation of business knowledge*? "Code is dead" misunderstands that it is the *web of human concerns the code represents*, and not the text on the screen, from which value is born. But this is a web! And a web is an *organic* mess — and need I remind you that not even our greatest robots can *unwind knots*? I welcome our new knitters, our new embroiderers…

Oh, look around! Are there not so many new portraits ready to be fashioned? But so few *painters*. Perhaps the best portrait I can provide, is that of the engineer that *paints their own portrait* — with enough of these, we may one day look forward to a new beautiful *landscape*. And you're afraid of this?!

## Notes

Our educators — that is to say, our leaders in tech — educate above all through the sterling image of human capacity they exhibit, and thereby prove possible. In this respect, they are doing an embarrassing job today. Who has a new image of software development, at the time it is most critical? I seek here to paint this new image that is dearly lacking from the public discourse — and I hope to do so not just with my fleeting words, but even more so with my conduct.
