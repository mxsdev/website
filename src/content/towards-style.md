Terence Tao calls this moment a [_"Copernican Revolution in intelligence"_](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26524). Apparently, intelligence was not as distinctively human or supreme as we once thought. How astonished I was to hear this! I had read the same point made by Nietzsche, writing over a century ago:

> _In some remote corner of the universe, poured out and glittering in countless solar systems, there was once a star on which clever beasts invented knowing. That was the most arrogant and mendacious minute of 'world history,' but nevertheless, it was only a minute._

Nietzsche understood that we are far, far more than intellects, and in so many of our activities, intellect plays a surprisingly small part. Granted, we are displaced from our old center — could the heavens now appear _larger_?

Software engineering is now an arbitrage — most senior "humans in the loop" are embarrassingly less valuable than their salaries suggest. It is the worst time possible to optimize for compensation and titles, because we have no clue what they will be in the next few years. If our engineers want a new _task_, it should be this: to use this reprieve to find a new voice compatible with this new future.

What new voices are available? The pro-AI voices are quite right that the anti-AI crowd are largely dissimulating their fear; but perhaps it _takes one to know one_. Is impetuously driving to rewrite everything in uncertain and untested technology any less driven by…_fear_? And fear of the same thing! What is certain: grand outcomes will precipitate no less from grand _courage_ in this new era than from the old one; and grand courage has no less its characteristic symptom of _equanimity_, a scarce commodity today.

Here is our engineers' latest path: _taste_! Value is now in our _craftsmanship_! [Taste is All That's Left](https://notashelf.dev/posts/taste-is-all-thats-left)! Apparently, taste is our ability to say "no" to agents, to stay headstrong in our "care". My friends, as romantic a concept as this is — do you really believe yourselves? Is this deliberative process not something agents have shown time and time again to excel at? Are you really ready to argue that our very _taste_ is not being fed to the machines, too? These _software sommeliers_, however exquisite, stand downstream of an already-existing product, passively ranking what they are handed; and perhaps it is this _passivity_ they are really defending with their illustrious _"taste"_.

What then, if not taste, are humans left with? It is none other than _creation_. You demand a definition of creation? Do you not see how a definition would foreclose the very novelty essential to creation? As long as we begin with _intellect_, as long as we start with the agent, as long as we treat "AI" as a product — we have already dived head-first into the river of un-creation. We are then yielding any possible advantage a human could have _even in principle_. Say "no" to an amalgamated average, however vigorously, you do not escape that average. The only winning move is not to play; but to invent a new play! To become the artist! To create not systems but _styles_!

Could there be style in _types and contracts_? Such a one will produce ingenious _invariants_ that solve these problems of security and determinism, as poets effect solemnity through the austerity of their verse. An artist of elegant constraints!

Could there be style in the _orchestration of agents_? It is the style of a great rhetorician who can mediate any argument, without either side feeling as though they lost. Our new politicians!

Could there be style in _representing our physical world_? They say art imitates nature after all! And is one's purpose not inseparable from one's style? "Context preparation is critical" — is this not an engineer's way of saying: it is beneficial to have more styles than realism! As Borges knew, a map as large as the territory is no map at all — our new _cartographers_!

Could there be style in _distillation of business knowledge_? "Code is dead" misunderstands that it is the web of human concerns the code represents, and not the text on the screen, from which value is born. But this is a web! And a web is an _organic_ mess which is _tended to_, not "solved"; I welcome our new _weavers_!

Oh, look around! Are there not so many new portraits ready to be fashioned? But so few _painters_. Perhaps the best portrait I can provide is that of the engineer who _paints their own portrait_ — with enough of these, we may one day look forward to a new beautiful _landscape_. And you're afraid of this? The creation of one's own style — this labor of choice, responsibility, and exposure — is thoroughly _unromantic_, and that is why we are so afraid.
